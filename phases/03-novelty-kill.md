# Phase 3 — Novelty Kill

## Purpose

Try hard to determine whether a candidate question is already answered, substantially undermined by prior work, or left with only an uninteresting residual.

Candidate death is a successful outcome.

## AI should

For each serious candidate:
- search through multiple independent literature routes when practical;
- run a separate very-recent search;
- identify the closest prior work, not merely broadly related papers;
- compare the candidate directly with what that work establishes;
- search for contrary results and known limitations;
- distinguish “exact experiment not done” from “scientific point already understood.”

Answer two separate questions:

1. Has the substantive question already been answered?
2. If not, would resolving the remaining uncertainty teach us something meaningful?

Present the closest evidence and its bearing on the candidate before giving a recommendation. In learning/collaborative mode, give the human a chance to form a provisional novelty judgment from that evidence; in an explicitly delegated review, provide the recommendation directly. Record which judgment was made before AI input when consequential.

## Independent review and verdict revision

Adversarial review means actively searching for counterevidence, not preferring a negative verdict. Keep the review target, scale and criteria fixed when comparing assessments; a different venue or standard is a separate assessment, not a correction by itself. Check critiques against the actual study and distinguish already-disclosed assumptions, genuine construct failures, and plausible untested alternatives. Apply the same evidential standard to claims for and against novelty and scientific value.

When using multiple AI reviewers, obtain independent assessments before sharing other reviewers' conclusions where practical. Separate factual corrections and supporting evidence from preferred recommendations. Treat reviews influenced by one another as dependent, not independent corroboration.

When revising a verdict, identify which claims the new evidence changes, which it leaves unchanged, and why the revised conclusion follows. Keep confidence proportional to the evidence; do not reverse or retain a verdict merely to align with a reviewer or the human.

## Human should

Read enough of the closest work to understand why it matters.

Then decide:
- CONTINUE;
- REFORMULATE;
- KILL;
- or remain BLOCKED pending evidence.

A reformulation is a new candidate, not a hidden rescue of the old one.

## Avoid

- treating keyword novelty as scientific novelty;
- trusting one retrieval system;
- letting fluent synthesis substitute for retrieval completeness;
- continuing merely because some technically distinct experiment exists.

## Exit condition

The surviving candidate has a clearly stated residual question, identified closest prior work, and a human judgment that answering the residual would still be scientifically worthwhile.
