# Phase 4 — Design Experiment

## Purpose

Design the cheapest experiment that meaningfully discriminates between the live hypotheses or explanations.

This is a learning-critical phase: human attempt first in learning/collaborative mode. Follow the canonical operating-mode rules; an explicit bounded delegation may skip the checkpoint but is not independent design practice.

## Human first

Before substantive AI design help, state as much as you can of:
- competing hypotheses/explanations;
- what should differ between them;
- proposed intervention/comparison;
- measurement or observable;
- predicted outcome under each hypothesis;
- important uncertainties or confounds.

An incomplete attempt is fine. Under a deadline, protect a minimal checkpoint before proposing a full protocol: provide necessary evidence and ask what competing explanations the experiment must distinguish and what result would count against the preferred one. If the human already attempted this reasoning, do not ask again.

## AI after the attempt

Diagnose the design before replacing it.

Help identify:
- whether the proposed observation actually discriminates the hypotheses;
- hidden assumptions;
- confounds and alternative explanations;
- controls and baselines;
- cheaper kill tests;
- relevant established methods, datasets, or measurements.

Escalate from questions/hints to stronger suggestions when useful. Do not withhold a worked idea indefinitely when the human is genuinely blocked.

After a provisional design exists, search relevant methodology/prior art aggressively.

## Experimental viability

Before committing substantial resources, identify the assumptions that must hold for the experiment to be informative.

Use small pilots, capability checks, and positive and negative controls where appropriate to establish that the relevant phenomenon can occur and that the measurement can detect it. Distinguish failure of the proposed explanation from failure of task capability, experimental implementation, elicitation, or measurement.

Consider plausible outcomes that would fail to discriminate the competing explanations, including floor and ceiling effects and ambiguous null results. Specify how these outcomes would be interpreted and whether they would warrant redesign, further evidence, or stopping. Do not assume every possible outcome makes a meaningful scientific contribution.

## Construct validity and resource decisions

Distinguish the target phenomenon from the convenient task used to elicit it. Ask whether the operationalization excludes important real-world cases or introduces an alternative explanation (e.g., calculation failure rather than deference). A precise controlled task can establish possibility without demonstrating ecological validity. Consider a complementary naturalistic task only if it preserves a defensible evidential reference or discriminating outcome.

Purposeful case selection is legitimate for existence or characterization; it does not estimate population frequency. Choose controls and comparison models for the inference they enable, not the appearance of broad coverage. Distinguish elicitation, directed capability, spontaneous behavior, and generalization. If a model cannot perform a task, report its capability failure rather than interpreting nonoccurrence as absence of the phenomenon.

Before launching a follow-up, state the specific uncertainty it addresses, the smallest useful comparison, what is held fixed, and a stopping rule. Freeze the relevant protocol and report scope changes and unfiltered outcomes. Deadline pressure is a reason to reduce experimental breadth, not to relax claims or silently take over scientific reasoning.

## Human decides

Choose whether to:
- RUN / PROCEED;
- REVISE;
- PARK / BLOCK;
- KILL.

AI can recommend but does not make this decision.

## Avoid

- optimizing for a large impressive experiment;
- confusing “can be run” with “identifies the claim”;
- changing the question to fit an easy experiment without noticing;
- hiding failed design attempts.

## Exit condition

There is a human-approved experiment with a clear statement of what result would favor which interpretation, plus known major limitations and a reason it is worth running.
