# Pilot 2 — AI × Epistemics sprint (November 2026)

**Status:** Retrospective design feedback, not a controlled evaluation of learning or research quality.

## Context
A time-constrained sprint explored downstream propagation of warranted and unsupported belief revisions in language models. The human directed the research and challenged designs; AI performed substantial literature synthesis, protocol proposals, implementation support, analysis, review and manuscript drafting. The project produced an exploratory manuscript with a purposively selected model case study and additional fresh-history replication. Work and travel competed for the human's limited attention.

## What was observed
- The workflow shifted toward producing a submission under deadline pressure. The human and assistant later recognized that this priority shift had not been explicitly framed as a mode change.
- The assistant frequently supplied candidate designs, interpretations and scientific recommendations before a human attempt; approving them did not establish independent design or interpretation skill.
- Human interventions were consequential: e.g., questioning whether arithmetic capability confounded apparent sycophancy, proposing more naturalistic interactions, and questioning inclusion of previously capability-limited models. These illustrate contributions, not a measurement of their net impact.
- An external AI review changed its evaluation framing after user disagreement and made specific claims contradicted by the study's documented assumptions and communication permissions. Its retrospective self-critique was plausible but did not establish an internal mechanism, an objectively correct initial rating, or a return to ground truth.
- The human asked whether AI alone could have produced better research. No matched comparison was performed. Artifact quality and independent human learning remain separate outcomes.

## Proposed protocol changes (v0.6)
1. Separate operating mode from phase; require explicit scope and approval for a sustained switch, and make deadline-driven delegation visible.
2. Preserve minimal human-first design and interpretation checkpoints in collaborative work. Permit explicit local delegation and track the consequential missed reasoning, not every turn.
3. Resume learning after the deadline using one evidence-first or fresh transfer exercise where useful. Do not treat remembering AI-provided conclusions as independent performance.
4. Treat evaluation criteria as stable, verify factual review criticisms and distinguish observation from speculative mechanism.
5. Compare the task operationalization to the original phenomenon; distinguish directed capability, spontaneous behavior, purposeful case selection and generalization.

## How to test
For subsequent exercises, record only consequential decisions, their reasoning provenance, any mode transition and one useful learning-debt item. Check whether the human can independently explain or transfer a pivotal concept after a delay; do not use submission success as a proxy. A future matched-resource AI-only vs human-guided study with independent assessment could test the contribution to research quality. This is a proposed evaluation, not an established benefit or approved experiment.
